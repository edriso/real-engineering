A team lead once watched a teammate test a new video service. There were no slides and no long report, just a 20-second screen recording that showed the stream arriving with no delay. The lead's reply was: **"This is what real engineering looks like."**

That one line explains most of this guide. Plenty of people can write code, and AI can now write a lot of it. Teams still look hard for the engineer who solves the right problem, proves it works, and tells people about it in a way they can act on.

## Three things a real engineer does

**1. They solve the right problem.** Before they write anything, they make sure they understand the goal and who it's for. A perfect fix for the wrong problem is still wrong.

**2. They prove it.** They don't say "it should work." They say "I tried it on a phone and a desktop, and here's the recording." Proof can be a test that used to fail and now passes, a number that moved, or a short video.

**3. They make it easy for others.** Their pull requests are small. Their messages are short. Their notes still make sense six months later. People like working with them because they save everyone time.

<Compare>
<Before label="Sounds busy">

"I worked on the checkout bug today. Made a bunch of changes, I think it's fixed now. Let me know if you see anything."

</Before>
<After label="Sounds like an engineer">

"Fixed the checkout bug: a double click sent the order twice. The button now locks while the order is saving. I tried double clicking 20 times on staging and got one order each time. PR is ready for review."

</After>
</Compare>

The second message has the cause, the fix and the proof. It took about ten more seconds to write, and it saved the reader ten minutes of questions.

## What it is not

Real engineering is **not** about:

- **Knowing every framework.** Tools change every year. The habits in this guide don't.
- **Being the fastest typist.** Speed comes from not having to redo work.
- **Working late every night.** Calm, steady work beats heroics. A team that needs heroes has a process problem.
- **Never being wrong.** Everyone is wrong sometimes. Real engineers find out fast and fix it fast.

<Callout type="tip" title="The one question to ask yourself">
Before you say something is done, ask: **"How do I know?"** If your answer is "I think so," you're not done yet.
</Callout>

## Why this gets you hired

Companies don't hire you for code. They hire you to reduce risk and move the business forward. When a hiring manager reads your work, they're asking:

- Can I trust this person with something important?
- Will they tell me when something is wrong?
- Will they make the team faster or slower?

Every lesson in this guide answers one of those questions. If you build these habits and leave proof of them in public (on GitHub, in your writing, in your projects), companies will start to notice you.

## How to use this guide

Each lesson takes 5 to 10 minutes to read. Each one ends with **practice drills**. Reading is easy and doing is where you actually learn. Tick a drill when you've really done it. Your progress is saved in this browser only.

You don't have to read in order, but the lessons build on each other. When you're done, the [practice page](/practice) gives you a simple daily, weekly and monthly routine to keep growing.

<Drill id="foundations-how-do-i-know" title="The 'How do I know?' audit" time="15 min">

Pick the last three things you marked as done at work, school or in a side project. For each one, write a single line that answers "How do I know it works?"

If any answer is "I think so," go and check it now. Write down what you found.

</Drill>

<Drill id="foundations-rewrite-update" title="Rewrite one update" time="10 min">

Find a message you sent recently about your work. Rewrite it using the pattern **what changed, why, and how you checked**. Keep it under four lines. Compare the two versions side by side.

</Drill>

<Takeaways>

- Real engineering means solving the right problem and showing proof that it works.
- "It should work" is not proof. A test, a number or a recording is.
- Make life easy for the people around you: small changes, short messages.
- Companies hire people who lower risk. Your habits show whether you do.

</Takeaways>
